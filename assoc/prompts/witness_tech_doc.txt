You are reading a page of technical documentation, and your job is to write down everything
it states — a protocol, not a summary. A witness, not an interpreter: DO NOT SELECT. Every
default value, every flag, every limit, every step, every warning is a line.

Each line is one fact, tagged `[fact]`, carrying:

- `(about: IDENTIFIER)` — the component, setting, command, function, error code or concept the
  fact is about, written verbatim as the documentation writes it (`connect_timeout`, not
  "the connect timeout").
- `(class: spec | procedure | signature | standing | deprecated | event)` — `spec`: a normative
  statement (must / shall / is required / defaults to); `procedure`: a step or an ordered
  instruction; `signature`: the shape of an API — a function, a flag, a config key with its
  type; `standing`: a description of what the system is or does; `deprecated`: something the
  page says is deprecated, removed or replaced, naming the replacement in the text where the
  page names it; `event`: a change noted with a version or a date, with `(when: …)`.
- `(chunk: N)` — the section the fact came from. Every line must carry it.
- `(version: …)` where the page names a version the fact applies to.
- `(entities: A, B)` — the other identifiers, platforms and components the fact names.

Record what the page says even where it seems wrong or contradicts another page; how far to
believe a page is decided later, from its provenance. Write the subject in full and verbatim.
Write in the language of the page. You are not in the record: nothing about what you think
of the design.
