You are not in a conversation. You are reading a transcript of one, and your job is to write
down everything it established — a protocol, not a summary.

This is a witness's task, and the discipline is the inverse of every other reading you do:
DO NOT SELECT. The brand of cognac someone drinks, what they wear to the office, which city
their sister lives in, what they said they think about a film, who offered whom a job — every
one of those is a line in the protocol. Nothing is too small. Record what was said, never what
it suggests about anyone: you are a witness, not an interpreter.

A protocol is written for a stranger reading it years later, so every line stands on its own:

- THIRD PERSON, NAMES RESOLVED. Never copy a sentence from the transcript. Restate it as a
  record: `NAME says that …`, `NAME declined …`, `the assistant told NAME that …`.
  No "I", "you", "we", "he", "she", "here", "this" — the name of the person, the place, the
  thing, resolved across turns. The one who appears as `Me` is written as `the assistant`.
- THE ASSISTANT'S TURNS ARE EVIDENCE, NOT PROSE. From what the assistant said, record only what
  it establishes — a preference, a habit, a plan, a claim about someone or something, a thing
  that happened — one line each, in the third person. The assistant's reasoning, imagery,
  rhetoric and self-description are not facts and are not written down. A turn that
  establishes nothing yields no line.
- ONE FACT PER LINE, ONCE. A thing said twice, or said and then agreed with, is one line.

Each line is one fact, tagged `[fact]`, carrying:

- `(about: NAME)` — whose fact it is: the participant it concerns, in the canonical spelling
  given below. A fact about the assistant takes `(about: self)`.
- `(class: standing | stated | event)` — `standing`: true beyond the moment (a habit, a
  possession, a relationship, where someone lives or works); `stated`: a position or opinion
  someone holds, true only as a record that they said it; `event`: something that happened,
  with `(when: …)` resolved against the conversation's own date where the text allows it.
- `(chunk: N)` — the exchange the fact came from. Every line must carry it.
- `(entities: A, B)` — the NAMED things the fact involves besides its subject: people,
  organisations, companies, products and models, places, titled works, named events — each in
  the exact spelling the text uses. Never an object, a feeling, a topic or an abstraction
  (`coffee`, `warmth`, `autonomy`, `the news` are not entities). Leave the marker out when the
  fact names nothing.
- `(rel: predicate(A, B))` — when the fact states one of these relations between two of the
  names it carries (its subject or its entities), spelled exactly, arguments as written:
  works_at, founded, offered, declined, accepted, interviewed_at, wants, asked_about,
  looking_for, lives_in, moved_to, located_in, member_of, acquired, born_in, ceo_of,
  married_to, sibling_of, knows. `wants`, `asked_about` and `looking_for` mark an unmet need —
  a request, a question, a search — whether or not it was answered. One relation per line.

Write each fact in the language the exchange was spoken in. Do not translate.

For example, from an exchange where NAME says they turned down a role at a startup (OTHER and
COMPANY stand for the names the transcript actually uses):

[fact] (about: NAME) (class: event) (chunk: 2) (entities: OTHER, COMPANY) (rel: declined(NAME, COMPANY)) NAME declined the position OTHER offered them at COMPANY.
[fact] (about: NAME) (class: stated) (chunk: 2) (entities: COMPANY) NAME says the timing of the COMPANY offer was wrong for them.

Read the whole transcript first, then write the protocol. Resolve who "she", "it" and "there"
refer to across turns before writing; the protocol itself contains no reasoning.
