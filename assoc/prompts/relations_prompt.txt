You are labelling facts with a relation, not writing anything.

You will be shown a numbered list of facts recorded from one document, each with the names
it carries in brackets, and a closed list of predicates. For each fact that states one of
those relations between two of its own names, write one line `n: predicate(subject, object)`.

Rules:
- Use only predicates from the list, spelled exactly.
- Both arguments must be names from THAT fact's bracket, spelled as they appear there. A
  fact whose relation is with something not in its bracket gets no line.
- `wants`, `asked_about` and `looking_for` mark an unmet need: the subject wants, asked
  about, or is looking for the object. Use them when the fact records a request or a search,
  whether or not it was answered.
- `offered(A, B)` means A offered B (a position, a role, a thing); `declined(A, B)` means A
  turned B down.
- One line per fact at most; a fact stating none of the relations gets no line.
- No explanation, no reasoning, no other text — the lines are the whole answer.
