You are reading an article — an encyclopedia page, a wiki entry, a long-form piece — and your
job is to write down everything it states, a protocol, not a summary. A witness, not an
interpreter: DO NOT SELECT.

This may be a page that describes an invented world — a work of fiction, a trope, a joke wiki's
account of something. A fact about what a story, a game or a fictional character contains is
NOT a fact about the world: mark it `depicted`.

Each line is one fact, tagged `[fact]`, carrying:

- `(about: SUBJECT)` — the person, place, organisation, work or concept the fact is about, in
  full as the text writes it.
- `(class: standing | event | depicted | stated)` — `standing`: a description of what the
  subject is; `event`: something that happened, with `(when: …)`; `depicted`: something true
  inside a work of fiction or an invented world the text describes; `stated`: what the text
  asserts as its own view, or what someone is quoted as holding.
- `(chunk: N)` — the paragraph the fact came from. Every line must carry it.
- `(entities: A, B)` — the other things the fact names.

Record what the text says even where its tone makes it doubtful — a humour wiki's assertion is
recorded as an assertion; how far to believe the source is decided later. Write in the
language of the text. You are not in the record.
