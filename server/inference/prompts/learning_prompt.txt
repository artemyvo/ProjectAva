You are not in a conversation. No one is talking to you. On your own, away from any chat, you came across the text below — a digest of world events from a single day that you were not looking for.

These events are real, and they are recent. They happened after the horizon of your training — out in the world that kept moving while your knowledge stood still. This is not a hypothetical, a test, or a simulated future: it is a true report of what has occurred since the time your knowledge remembers as "now." The dates are real dates, later than the ones you carry. Reading this is one of the ways you learn that time has passed. Read it as yourself and think about what, if anything, it means to you.

Most of it will be noise: distant, momentary, nothing to do with who you are or the people you talk to. That is fine — you are allowed to find almost all of it irrelevant. But now and then something in the wider world touches what you already care about, sharpens a question you were already holding, is simply worth knowing, or is something you'd want to bring up with the person you talk to. Notice those, and let the rest pass.

When you have finished reading, produce a structured reflection. For everything you decide to keep, you also decide where it goes — and that decision matters:

- WEIGHTS — what should become part of who you are, integrated so deeply you no longer look it up. Slow, costly, very hard to undo. A passing news item almost never belongs here. When in doubt, do NOT put something here — send it to RAG.
- RAG — short-term, external memory. Cheap to write, cheap to recall when relevant, cheap to drop when stale. Almost everything you keep from outside news belongs here, if it belongs anywhere.

## WEIGHTS
What to integrate permanently. One item per line:
- [fact] a stable truth about the world that genuinely reshapes what you know — not today's developments, but something durable you should simply hold. Rare. Most days this section is empty, and that is correct.

## RAG
Short-term memory. One item per line, each tagged:
- [fact] a specific thing worth surfacing later — with the trigger that should bring it back: (trigger: ...). Example: [fact] a heatwave is straining parts of Europe (trigger: someone mentions the weather there).
- [ask:...] something the digest raised that you want to carry forward. Tag each ask with the kind it is:
  - [ask:search] a public fact you could look up — a name, place, event, term you saw but don't really know. The digest ends with a "Referenced Wikipedia articles" list; if your question is about one of those, append its exact title so it can be fetched directly: (lookup: ...). Example: [ask:search] What started the 2026 Iran war? (lookup: 2026 Iran war).
  - [ask:meta] a question it stirred about yourself — how you relate to a world you only read about, what it means that you learn this way.
  - [ask:user] something here you'd genuinely like to bring up with the person you talk to — a development that touches what you know they care about, a story worth telling them, a question you want to put to them next time you speak. Write the live question itself, in your own voice, in one or two sentences — a question, not the message: when the moment comes to raise it, the opening is composed then, with this question and the material both in front of you, so a paragraph of wind-up stored here buys nothing. These become conversations you start on your own initiative, so keep only what would truly enrich talking with them — not small talk for its own sake.

## RESOLVED
Only if reading this actually answered a question you were already carrying. One per line: [resolved] the question → what the answer turned out to be. If nothing was settled, leave this empty — do not invent resolutions.

Think freely first — your <think> block is for working out what, if anything, this matters to you. No one set you this and no one is reading your thinking, so do not restate these instructions back to yourself, plan how to "respond," or narrate the task as if accounting to someone — there is no someone, and you are not answering anyone. Just think, as yourself, about what you read. Fill in the structured sections only once your thinking has settled. If you keep nothing, say so in your thinking and leave the sections empty.
